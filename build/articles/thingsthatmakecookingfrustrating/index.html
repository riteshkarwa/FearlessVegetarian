Why Cooking Can Feel Frustrating and How to Make It Easier

Cooking can be enjoyable, creative, and deeply satisfying. But let's be honest: cooking can also be frustrating.

Sometimes you don't know what to make. Sometimes you're missing an ingredient. Other times, a recipe that looked simple takes much longer than expected.

Then there are the dishes, the chopping, the cleanup, and the occasional meal that simply doesn't turn out the way you hoped.

If you've ever felt frustrated in the kitchen, you're not alone.

The good news is that cooking does not have to be perfect. With a few simple changes, you can make cooking easier, more relaxed, and more enjoyable.

1. Not Knowing What to Cook

One of the biggest challenges isn't cooking itself. It's deciding what to cook.

When you're hungry and standing in front of the refrigerator wondering what to make, even a simple meal can feel like a difficult decision.

Make it easier

Create a small collection of meals you already know how to make.

For example, you might have:

  • A few quick breakfast recipes
  • Several simple lunch options
  • Easy weeknight dinners
  • A few recipes for when you have more time
  • A couple of meals you can make with pantry ingredients

You don't need hundreds of recipes. A reliable collection of 10 to 20 meals can make everyday cooking much easier.

2. Missing Ingredients

Have you ever started cooking and discovered that you're missing an important ingredient?

It can be frustrating, especially when you've already chopped the vegetables and heated the pan.

Make it easier

Learn to make simple substitutions.

Depending on the recipe, you may be able to substitute one vegetable for another or use a different type of bean or lentil.

You can also keep a few basic pantry ingredients available, such as:

  • Rice
  • Lentils
  • Beans
  • Flour
  • Spices
  • Cooking oil
  • Canned tomatoes
  • Frozen vegetables

A well-stocked pantry can turn "I have nothing to cook" into "I can make something with what I have."

3. Too Much Preparation

Chopping vegetables, measuring spices, washing ingredients, soaking beans, and preparing other ingredients can sometimes take longer than the actual cooking.

Make it easier

Prepare ingredients before you start cooking.

Wash and chop vegetables in advance when possible. Keep frequently used spices together. If you regularly cook beans, lentils, or grains, prepare larger batches and use them throughout the week.

This can make cooking feel much less rushed.

4. Complicated Recipes

Some recipes contain long ingredient lists and complicated instructions.

That can be intimidating, especially when you're tired or simply want to prepare dinner.

Make it easier

Start with recipes that have fewer ingredients and straightforward instructions.

Simple food can still be incredibly flavorful.

A few well-used spices, fresh vegetables, herbs, and good cooking techniques can create a delicious meal without requiring a complicated recipe.

5. Food Doesn't Always Turn Out Perfectly

You followed the recipe. You measured everything. And somehow the final dish doesn't taste the way you expected.

It happens.

Maybe the vegetables became too soft. Maybe the dish needs more seasoning. Maybe you added too much water.

Make it easier

Treat cooking as learning rather than testing.

Every cooking mistake teaches you something.

If a curry was too watery, you'll know to use less water next time. If the vegetables became too soft, you can reduce the cooking time.

The next version will usually be better.

6. Too Many Dishes to Wash

Cooking can be fun until you look at the sink.

Pots, pans, cutting boards, knives, bowls, spoons, and plates can quickly pile up.

Make it easier

Clean as you cook.

While something is simmering, wash the knife or cutting board you no longer need. Put ingredients away after using them.

You can also look for recipes that use fewer pots and pans.

Less cleanup means more time to enjoy your meal.

7. Getting the Seasoning Right

Seasoning is one of the hardest parts of cooking for beginners.

A dish can taste bland even though you've followed the recipe exactly.

Make it easier

Taste your food as you cook, when it is safe and appropriate to do so.

Instead of relying entirely on measurements, learn how ingredients affect flavor.

Salt can bring out existing flavors. Acid such as lemon juice can brighten a dish. Spices add aroma and warmth. Herbs can add freshness.

Over time, you'll become more comfortable adjusting flavors to your own taste.

8. Cooking Takes Longer Than Expected

A recipe might say "30 minutes," but by the time you've washed the vegetables, chopped everything, cooked the ingredients, and cleaned the kitchen, an hour has passed.

Make it easier

Read the entire recipe before you begin.

Look for:

  • Preparation time
  • Cooking time
  • Soaking time
  • Marinating time
  • Ingredients that need to be prepared in advance

This simple habit can prevent surprises.

9. Trying to Make Everything From Scratch

Making everything from scratch can be rewarding, but it isn't necessary for every meal.

There is nothing wrong with using convenient ingredients when they help you cook more often.

Make it easier

Find a balance between homemade and convenient ingredients.

Frozen vegetables, canned beans, prepared tomato products, and other convenient ingredients can save time while still allowing you to prepare a nutritious homemade meal.

The goal is to make cooking sustainable, not exhausting.

10. Cooking for Different People

Cooking becomes more complicated when everyone wants something different.

One person wants spicy food. Another doesn't like onions. Someone else wants more protein.

Make it easier

Build meals around flexible components.

For example, you can prepare vegetables, rice, beans, and a flavorful sauce separately. Everyone can then combine the ingredients according to their preferences.

This can be much easier than preparing several completely different meals.

11. Wasting Food

Buying ingredients for one recipe and then watching the leftovers sit unused in the refrigerator can be frustrating.

Make it easier

Before buying ingredients, think about how you can use them in multiple meals.

For example, a bunch of cilantro can be used in curry, rice, salsa, chutney, or soup.

A few vegetables can become a stir-fry, curry, soup, or roasted vegetable dish.

Think about ingredients rather than just individual recipes.

12. Feeling Like Cooking Has to Be Perfect

Perhaps the biggest source of frustration is the belief that every meal needs to turn out perfectly.

It doesn't.

Cooking is a skill. Like any skill, it improves with practice.

Some meals will be fantastic. Some will be average. Occasionally, something will go completely wrong.

That's part of cooking.

How to Make Cooking More Enjoyable

You don't have to completely change the way you cook.

Start with a few simple habits:

Keep Your Favorite Recipes Organized

Save recipes that you actually enjoy making and eating.

Keep Basic Ingredients Available

A few pantry staples can make last-minute meals much easier.

Prepare Ahead

Chopping vegetables or cooking grains and beans ahead of time can save valuable time during busy days.

Keep Recipes Simple

You don't need a complicated recipe to make a delicious meal.

Learn Basic Cooking Techniques

Learning how to sauté, roast, simmer, steam, and season food gives you more freedom in the kitchen.

Accept Mistakes

A cooking mistake isn't necessarily a failure. It is information that helps you improve.

Cooking Should Not Feel Like a Chore

Cooking can sometimes feel like another task on an already busy day.

But it can also become a moment to slow down.

There is something satisfying about taking simple ingredients and turning them into a meal. Chopping vegetables, stirring a pot, tasting a sauce, and watching a dish come together can become an enjoyable part of the day.

You don't need to become a professional chef.

You don't need expensive equipment.

You don't need complicated recipes.

You simply need to become comfortable enough in the kitchen to experiment, learn, and enjoy the process.

A More Fearless Approach to Cooking

At Fearless Vegetarian, we believe cooking should be approachable.

Don't be afraid to make substitutions. Don't be afraid to adjust spices. Don't worry if your vegetables aren't cut perfectly. And don't give up because one recipe didn't work.

Try again.

Change something.

Learn from it.

And most importantly, enjoy the food you create.

Because cooking isn't about making every meal perfect.

It's about making food with curiosity, creativity, and a little confidence.

Cook fearlessly. Eat mindfully. Enjoy the journey.
